## Connected booths, invisible data

Weinmann Technologies builds **connected** industrial paint booths. They report data (consumption, statuses, alerts), but customers had no direct access to it: to find out how their equipment was doing, they had to go through after-sales support.

The Client Portal is the answer to that problem, and the subject of my **final-year engineering project**, completed as a work-study student at Weinmann. I carried it **alone, end to end**: from defining the need to delivery.

## Running the project, not just coding it

Before the first line of code, there was a project to frame. I handled its management entirely:

- **gathering requirements** from Weinmann's teams and writing them up as a **requirements specification**;
- **organising and planning** the work, from breaking it into stages to setting delivery priorities;
- **delivering** the portal and presenting it at my final-year defence.

Specification, organisation, design, front-end and back-end development, deployment: every hat on this project was mine.
</Callout>

## Collect

The first technical building block: automatically gather the equipment data and store it in a usable way, booth by booth, customer by customer.

## Show

Customers check their booths' status in real time through **dashboards**: consumption, performance indicators, history and alerts. The goal: a workshop manager understands the situation in a few seconds, without an export or a phone call.

## Separate

A customer portal means many customers on the same platform. The architecture is **multi-tenant from the start**: each customer has their own access rights and only sees their own equipment.

<Callout title="Delivered">
The portal was delivered and presented as my final-year engineering project. It lets Weinmann offer a modern customer experience around its connected equipment, and opens the door to new digital services.
</Callout>

## Stack

Next.js and TypeScript, TailwindCSS, a Node.js back end with MySQL, hosted on a Linux VPS.
