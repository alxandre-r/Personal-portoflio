## Connected booths, invisible data

Weinmann Technologies builds **connected** industrial paint booths. They report data (consumption, statuses, alerts), but customers had no direct access to it: to find out how their equipment was doing, they had to go through after-sales support.

The Client Portal is the answer to that problem, and the subject of my **final-year engineering project**, completed as a work-study student at Weinmann. I carried it **alone, end to end**: from defining the need to delivery.

## Collect

The first technical building block: get each booth's data into a usable database, reliably and securely.

- **validation rules right at the PLC**, plus handling of transmission errors, so only clean data gets stored;
- an **HTTPS link** between each PLC and the API, with automatically renewed TLS certificates;
- a **Django REST API**, authenticated with **JWT**, dedicated to ingestion: it receives the PLC data and writes it to the database;
- a **dedicated MySQL database** for the booths: an indexed relational schema, with **views and stored procedures** to aggregate consumption without slowing the site down.

The real challenge was finding the right pace: data fresh enough to be useful, without flooding the server with requests.

## Show

Customers check their booths' status in real time through **dashboards**: consumption, performance indicators, history and alerts. The goal: a workshop manager understands the situation in a few seconds, without an export or a phone call.

The screens were first mocked up in **Figma**, then built in Next.js: charts, tables and filters. The portal reads its data through **Next.js's built-in API routes**, kept separate from the ingestion API.

## Separate

A customer portal means many customers on the same platform. The architecture is **multi-tenant from the start**: each customer has their own access rights and only sees their own equipment.

Authentication has several levels (customer, user, administrator), and each customer manages their own users and administrators, **site by site**. The role system is designed to grow without a rewrite.

## The after-sales back office

On Weinmann's side, the customer service team has its own secure interface:

- creating and editing customers, their sites and their equipment;
- granting and revoking user rights;
- maintenance indicators: connection status, error logs, alerts.

## Stack

The portal is built with Next.js and TypeScript with TailwindCSS, and its built-in API routes read the data from MySQL. A Django (Python) API takes care of writing the booth data into it. Everything is hosted on a Linux VPS.
